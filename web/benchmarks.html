Expected differences between YAMC and OpenMC

Some benchmarks show a difference that is expected: it comes from a methodological choice, not from either code being wrong. Read these before treating a disagreement as an error.

Depletion chain reduction (transmutation benchmarks)

YAMC and OpenMC interpret the depletion "reduce" level differently, so low-density nuclides can differ. YAMC's reduce_transport_chain bounds only which product nuclides get cross sections loaded for neutron transport; the burnup (Bateman) equations are always solved on the full decay network. That solve is cheap (sparse CRAM) and exact, so nothing is saved by trimming it and no accuracy is lost by keeping it. OpenMC's Chain.reduce instead prunes the whole network (transport, decay, and the Bateman matrix together), so at a low level it drops decay-fed nuclides entirely.

The consequence is analytic, not a coincidence: any nuclide that appears in YAMC's result but lies outside OpenMC's reduced chain is one OpenMC cannot produce by construction. Where the codes disagree on such a low-density, decay-fed nuclide, YAMC is the more complete result and OpenMC has truncated a real decay pathway. It is not YAMC being wrong. The dominant, physically significant nuclides agree closely; the disagreements sit orders of magnitude below them.

Shutdown dose rate / decay photons (D1S benchmarks)

For decay-gamma and shutdown-dose-rate benchmarks, YAMC's D1S (Direct-1-Step) also emits the decay gammas of the decay products: it follows the activation daughters down the chain and includes their decay photons. OpenMC's D1S attributes decay gammas only to the directly activated nuclide and misses the gammas emitted by those subsequent decay products. Expect YAMC to show additional decay-photon flux and lines from the daughter nuclides; that extra signal is real physics OpenMC's D1S omits.

Photon heating / KERMA tallies (secondary-photon benchmarks)

Wherever photon heating is compared (the secondary-photon benchmarks), neither code transports electrons or positrons, so the energy those particles would carry is deposited locally instead. YAMC folds that electron and positron energy into its photon heating tally (photon KERMA). OpenMC deposits the same energy but books it separately, under its own electron and positron particle filters, so an OpenMC photon-only heating tally is lower by that amount.

To compare heating like for like, the verification sums OpenMC's photon, electron, and positron heating tallies and compares that total against YAMC's single photon-heating number. The two are then equivalent. A raw YAMC-photon vs OpenMC-photon-only comparison would differ by the electron and positron contribution, which is a bookkeeping difference, not a physics one.

Compton-ionization fluorescence (photon benchmarks)

When a Compton scatter ionizes a bound shell, the vacancy relaxes and emits a characteristic fluorescence X-ray (Fe K-alpha 6.4 keV, Cu 8.0 keV, W/Pb L and K lines). Both codes emit these only if they can match each Compton-profile shell to its atomic-relaxation subshell, and the two ENDF/B-VIII.1 tables come from different evaluations (Biggs Compton profiles vs EADL relaxation) whose binding energies for the same shell disagree by ~0.5-1%. OpenMC matches them by binding energy within a 1e-5 tolerance, when deciding whether to emit a fluorescence photon.

YAMC instead associates the shells by electron occupancy at data-generation time (a Compton (n, l) shell maps to its constituent (n, l, j) subshells), so it emits the real characteristic lines. Expect YAMC to show extra flux (typically a few tens of percent) in the low-energy line bins for mid/high-Z elements.
